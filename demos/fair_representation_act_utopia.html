What if every House district elected 3–5 members?
#30DayMapChallenge · Day 8: Utopia

Drag the divider. On the left are today's single-member U.S. House districts, where one party usually wins every seat. On the right is FairVote's sample plan under the Fair Representation Act: the House keeps its 435 members, but states with six or more seats elect 3–5 representatives per district by proportional ranked choice voting, and smaller states elect everyone at large. Colors show the projected party mix of each district's seats, so purple means a district sends both parties to the House.

What about the Senate?

The Fair Representation Act only changes elections to the U.S. House. The Senate stays as it is: each state still elects two senators statewide, in separate single-seat races held in different years, so there are no Senate districts to redraw and the bill doesn't change them.
